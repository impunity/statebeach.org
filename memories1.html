ONE TIME IN RUSSIA, 1985

In the spring of 1985, before glasnost and perestroika, Tom, JP, and I decided it would be great fun to get into mischief behind the Iron Curtain. One morning in Kiev, we met a dissident named Yuri who was quite helpful to us. At some point, he explained that we could do him a big favor if we would buy a Sony mini stereo for him. Such goods were not available to Soviet citizens. They were available only in beriozkas, special stores for westerners where Soviet citizens were not allowed to enter. He had seen the stereo through the window and dreamed of owning it. Of course, such a purchase was absolutely forbidden and would have to accomplished secretly or Yuri could soon be cutting trees in Siberia. Yuri then outlined an involved plan for the purchase and transfer of the item. It was obvious he had been planning this for a long time and had been waiting for just the right chumps, gullible and foolish enough, to run the risk of such a deal. We exceeded his wildest imagination. We then followed his plan, purchased the Sony, and met him, his wife and their small child at a public event, and then proceeded back to their apartment when it was dark and we were unlikely to be seen. When we got into the apartment, Yuri and his wife were ecstatic. We came appreciate how important such a small luxury was in such a repressed country. What little they did have was crummy. To own a small Sony stereo, about $100 (and their entire life savings), seemed to be one of the greatest events of their life. Yuri thanked us effusively and gave us gifts in exchange. Tom, holding out the wrapped package, returned the effusive thanks and told Yuri that we liked him so much that we had not gotten the Sony. We had gotten "something better." Tom then handed the package to Yuri saying, "We got you the Polish stereo instead!" Tears rolled down Yuri's cheeks and it was some minutes before his heart beat returned to normal as Tom explained he was only kidding.

- Bonner
